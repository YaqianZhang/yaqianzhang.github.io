Biological neural networks are natural lifelong learners. They continually acquire new skills, consolidate what they learn, and adapt to ever-changing circumstances. For artificial neural networks, learning something new can unexpectedly disrupt what a model already knows, often in ways that are hard to predict.

I want to understand how intelligent systems learn and grow from incoming experience and data. What determines whether new learning builds on existing knowledge or interferes with it? When is forgetting a failure, and when is it a necessary part of adaptation? And as AI systems move from static deployment to continual adaptation, how can we keep them predictable, controllable, and beneficial to the people who interact with them?

My work brings together three closely related research areas:

- **Graceful Continual Adaptation:** Understanding when and why forgetting occurs, and developing methods that allow AI systems to learn new knowledge and capabilities while _selectively_ preserving or revising existing ones. I am particularly interested in making continual adaptation more predictable and controllable.

- **Sample-Efficient Reinforcement Learning:** Developing statistically and computationally efficient algorithms that learn from interaction and feedback. My work explores policy gradient methods and decision-making under limited feedback, particularly in settings where collecting interaction data is costly.

- **Human–AI Interaction:** Understanding how people interact with and adapt to intelligent systems, and designing AI systems that respond to users' changing needs and behaviours. My work includes interdisciplinary research on adaptive learning environments, intelligent tutoring systems, and human-centred applications.
